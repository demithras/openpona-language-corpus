## Matrix invariants

- 42 cells, no duplicates.
- The Structure column is exactly the six structural tokens.
- Column names are functional (Seed, Map, Explore, Decide, Work, Resonate, Structure) and carry the Lift names C1–C7 (Identity, Ground, Transform, Select, Realize, Evaluate, Structure); the rows carry the Lift names R1–R6 (Process, Inquiry, Method, Agency, Representation, Integration). A planetary mnemonic (Sun … Saturn) is kept in `LEARN_OPENPONA.md`, Lesson 1, and `history/`; it is not canon, not evidence and not vocabulary (decision 2026-10-01).
- The first cell is `open`; the final cell is `anu`.
- `sike` marks cyclic/recurrent semantics near the start of the final row; the sequence itself may be traversed cyclically in mnemonic use.
- The Lift labels are canonical external axis names (author decision 2026-10-09): metadata about the matrix, never tokens, never part of the surface, never new primitives. The 42-token inventory (`anu 1.1`) and the placement of `anu` in the last cell are unchanged and there is no token-version bump. That the labels GENERATE the tokens is research, not canon.
- The labels in this table are the labels of `research/lift/coordinates.v0.1.json` (`row_archetypes`, `column_operations`); `tests/test_canon.py` checks that they agree and that the table's 42 tokens are exactly `data/matrix.csv`.

## Embedded headers vs literal headers

Internal research (`research/matrix_coordinate_experiments.md`, MX1–MX3; unpublished) rejected the simplistic idea that only one edge of the matrix is a type-lift/header. Both axes appear operator-like. A stronger current research model treats row and column semantics as external coordinates whose intersections attract lexical tokens; the names of those coordinates (the Lift labels above) are canon since 2026-10-09, the claim that they generate the tokens is not.

The first row/first column can act as **embedded projections** of those axes, but they are still data cells, not formal headers. This distinction matters because exact generation of all 42 tokens from axes alone is not yet proven.

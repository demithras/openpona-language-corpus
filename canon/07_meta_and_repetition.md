# 07 — META and repetition algebra

**Status: CANON rule; semantic gloss remains contextual.**

Repetition is not decorative emphasis. It creates a higher-order semantic derivative.

Let `D(P)` denote one META application over a valid semantic unit `P`.

```text
P P  => D(P)
```

Nested derivatives compose additively:

```text
D^m(D^n(P)) = D^(m+n)(P)
```

Consequences:

- `n` repetitions of `P` denote `D^(n-1)(P)`; four repetitions are `D^3(P)`;
- only the resulting depth is meaningful: `D^2(D^2(P)) = D^4(P)`, as snap is the acceleration of acceleration, and there is no separate surface form for a nested derivative;
- META recognition has precedence over a structural interpretation that would incorrectly split the repeated unit, but only where the folded reading is valid: if the fold makes the statement invalid, the unfolded reading is used (`jan pi ilo ilo` is RESOLVED unfolded; author decision 2026-10-09);
- priority combines lexicographically over the valid readings: first the readings with a maximal-by-inclusion set of folded runs, then, among those, the readings with a maximal-by-inclusion set of structural `tan` positions; one survivor is RESOLVED, several incomparable survivors are AMBIGUOUS (author decision 2026-10-09; SPEC invariant 18, §7.2);
- the language does not assign one fixed English word such as "meta" or "essence" to every depth; the operational interpretation is contextual.

This rule turns repetition into a compact reflexive mechanism and motivated the research view of OpenPona as a linear notation for a reflexive semantic graph. Whether that notation is sufficient for a full typed ontology language is tested separately.
